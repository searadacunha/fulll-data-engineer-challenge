| Last start | 2017-06-30 23:59:01 | 2017-06-30 23:52:44 | 2017-06-30 12:59:36 |
| Trips ending in July | 14 | 10 | 16 |

Every timestamp of the files is ISO-like, so none is ambiguous. The statement prints dates day first (`23/06/2017 15:09:32`): that example is the first row of `chicago.csv` written another way. The program accepts these two layouts and no other, without any inference, so that a day and a month can never be swapped silently (`03/04/2017` is 3 April; `06/23/2017` is refused). A value in any other layout becomes missing and is counted in the report.

Difference between the `Trip Duration` column and the time elapsed between the two timestamps, in trips:

| Difference | Chicago | New York City | Washington |
|---|---|---|---|
| within 1 s | 299,998 | 300,000 | 187,082 |
| 1 s to 1 min | 0 | 0 | 107,187 |
| about 1 h | 2 | 0 | 1 |
| about 12 h | 0 | 0 | 5,096 |
| about 24 h | 0 | 0 | 634 |
| other | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| End before start | 0 | 0 | 4,878 |

- Trips one hour apart (Chicago 2 and Washington 1): all of them span 2:00 on 12 March 2017, when clocks moved forward an hour. The duration is right; the timestamps are local times.
- New York City: every duration matches the elapsed time to within a second.
- Washington: timestamps of January to March are at whole minutes (100% of them end in `:00`, against 1.7% from April), hence the differences under a minute. The larger ones come from the clock described below.

The program therefore uses the `Trip Duration` column for travel times, never `End Time - Start Time`.

## Washington: a 12-hour clock from April

Start hours found in January to March: 0 to 23 (24 distinct values). From April to June: 1 to 12 only (12 distinct values). From April the hours were written on a 12-hour clock and the AM/PM mark was lost: 08:15 can be a morning or an evening trip. This also explains the end times before start times and the gaps of about 12 and 24 hours above.

Dates are not affected, so months and days of the week stay right. The hour of day is: the most common start hour of Washington is computed on the hours as recorded, and [results.md](results.md) also gives it on January to March alone. Nothing in the data can restore the lost AM/PM, so the program does not try.

No trip is shorter than a minute in any file, which suggests the sample excludes such trips. Trips of several days are real records (a bike not docked, for instance); they are kept, since the statement asks for the total and the mean of what was recorded. Their weight on the mean is shown in [results.md](results.md). The program would leave out a missing, non-numeric, infinite or negative duration and count it.

## What the statement says and what the files hold

- *Start Time (e.g., 23/06/2017 15:09:32)*: the files use `2017-06-23 15:09:32`.
- *Trip Duration (in seconds - e.g., 321)*: Washington stores decimals (`489.066`).
- *Randomly selected data from capitalbikeshare.com*: Capital Bikeshare is the Washington system; the Chicago and New York City trips come from those cities' own systems, Divvy and Citi Bike (see [references.md](references.md) for their terms).
- *First six months of 2017*: true for start times; a few trips end on 1 July or later.
